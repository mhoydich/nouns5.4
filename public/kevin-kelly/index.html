A tribute

Kevin Kelly.

Photographer, editor, cataloger of tools, co-founder of Wired, and the person who wrote the field manual for the networked world before most of us had email.

The book: New Rules for the New Economy (1998)

01 / The one book

New Rules for the New Economy

In 1998 the web was a few years old, most businesses treated it as a brochure, and "the network" still sounded like a TV term. Kelly published a short book arguing that connection itself was rewriting economics, and that the winners would be the people who learned the new rules first.

It reads less like a forecast and more like a set of habits. Value comes from the network, not the node. Generosity compounds. Control loosens at the top. Change is the steady state. Nearly three decades later, the list still works as a checklist for anyone building something.

Its quiet lesson: when the cost of copying, connecting and computing falls toward zero, the scarce thing is what people choose to point it at.

The chapters, as Kelly titled them:

  1. 01

    Embrace the swarm

    Power comes from many small connected parts, not one big center.

  2. 02

    Increasing returns

    In a network, value grows faster than size. Early momentum feeds itself.

  3. 03

    Plentitude, not scarcity

    The more copies of a thing there are, the more each one is worth.

  4. 04

    Follow the free

    Prices drift toward zero, so give the copy away and charge for what can't be copied.

  5. 05

    Feed the web first

    Grow the shared network before you grow your own slice of it.

  6. 06

    Let go at the top

    Abandon what's working while it still works, before the market does it for you.

  7. 07

    From places to spaces

    Geography matters less. Distance between buyer and maker collapses.

  8. 08

    No harmony, all flux

    Constant churn is the healthy state, not a crisis to wait out.

  9. 09

    Relationship tech

    The network's real product is trust and relationship, not the transaction.

  10. 10

    Opportunities before efficiencies

    Don't solve problems faster. Find the new things that only now become possible.

Short summaries above are PointCast's own paraphrase, not Kelly's text. He keeps the whole book free to read on his site, which is very on brand: read it at kk.org.

02 / Why it stuck

A book you keep re-reading at each new wave.

Read it in 1998 and it's about the web. Read it in 2008 and it's about social and mobile. Read it in 2021 and it's about tokens and DAOs. Read it today and it's about AI: intelligence becoming the cheap, abundant thing, and coordination becoming the scarce one.

That's the mark of a durable idea. The rules weren't about a specific technology. They were about what happens to people and markets when connection gets cheap, and connection keeps getting cheaper.

Kelly's later essays carry the same optimism. "1,000 True Fans" says you don't need millions, just a real core who love what you make. "You Are Not Late" says the great things haven't been built yet, and the person starting today is still early. Both are, in a way, chapter eleven of the same book.

03 / A short history

The life behind the rules

Kelly has no college degree. He did a year of geology at the University of Rhode Island, then went to Asia with a camera. Most of what he knows he learned by walking, building, cataloging and asking.

  • 1952Born in Pennsylvania. Graduates high school in Westfield, New Jersey, in 1970.
  • 1972–79Independent photographer roaming remote Asia, from Japan and Burma to Nepal, Afghanistan and Iran.
  • 1979Bicycles about 5,000 miles across the United States.
  • 1984–90Publisher and editor of the Whole Earth Review, working alongside Stewart Brand on the Whole Earth Catalogs.
  • 1984Helps start the Hackers Conference, gathering three generations of computer pioneers in one room.
  • 1985Part of the launch of the WELL, one of the first online communities, and sits on its board until 1998.
  • 1993Co-founds Wired and serves as founding executive editor through 1999. Today his title there is Senior Maverick.
  • 1994Out of Control: networks, swarms and decentralized systems, years before those words were common.
  • 1995Charter board member of the Long Now Foundation, home of the 10,000-year clock.
  • 1998New Rules for the New Economy.
  • 2003Launches Cool Tools, a reader-written catalog of things that work. Still going.
  • 2010What Technology Wants and the idea of the technium.
  • 2016The Inevitable: twelve forces shaping the next thirty years.
  • 2021Vanishing Asia, a 1,000-page photo record of fading traditions, funded on Kickstarter.
  • 2023Excellent Advice for Living, 450 bits of wisdom he wished he'd known earlier.

He lives in Pacifica, a small coastal town just south of San Francisco, and still publishes almost everything he makes in the open.

04 / Why he's cool

A techno-optimist who mostly uses a bike, a camera and a notebook.

He helped invent the vocabulary of the internet age and then spent decades documenting places that technology hadn't reached. He runs a tools catalog, not a startup. He gives his books away online. He writes advice for his kids and publishes it for everyone.

The posture is the lesson: be early, be generous, stay curious, and assume the best things haven't been made yet.

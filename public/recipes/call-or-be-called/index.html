AI Recipes 001
Call or be called.

Eight practical recipes for AI phone and voice agents. Each one lists its ingredients, its steps, and the thing that goes wrong. The stack facts and calling rules were checked on the vendors' and regulators' own pages on November 8, 2026.

Why the phone, why now.

Call, or be called

Real-time voice models now pick up a phone number directly. OpenAI's Realtime API takes SIP calls, Twilio relays speech as text to your app, and open frameworks like Pipecat and LiveKit run the whole loop on your own servers. A product helpline that knows which bottle the caller is holding is a weekend build. Doing it well is the job.

Two directions matter. Inbound: someone calls and the line answers like your best employee. Outbound: the line calls them, which the law treats very differently. And one format is worth stealing from game shows: the call people hope to get.

03 / THE RECIPES

Eight levels. Save your game often.

LEVEL 1 / INBOUND / DIFFICULTY ★★☆☆☆

The Product Line

INGREDIENTS

  • One phone number (Twilio, LiveKit, Retell or a SIP trunk)
  • One real-time voice engine from the pantry
  • Your product catalog as a tool: names, nicknames, what's printed on the label
  • A short system prompt with the brand's voice and its limits
  • A transcript log and a human on call

STEPS

  1. Answer with who you are and that the caller is speaking with an AI.
  2. Ask what they're holding. Match it with a catalog lookup tool, not the model's memory.
  3. Confirm the match out loud before answering anything about it.
  4. Answer only from the catalog and an approved FAQ. Anything else goes to a human.
  5. End with a one-line recap and log the call.

WATCH OUTPeople say "the green one" and "that gummy from Tuesday". Put nicknames and label text in the catalog, and when two products match, ask.

Serves: one brand, all day

LEVEL 2 / LATENCY / DIFFICULTY ★★★☆☆

The Speed Run

INGREDIENTS

  • A latency budget for each step, written down
  • Streaming everywhere: speech-to-text, model tokens, voice
  • A region close to your callers and your phone carrier
  • A stopwatch: measure mouth to ear, not server time

STEPS

  1. Start from Twilio's published baseline: about 1.1 seconds median mouth to ear for a cascaded agent, 1.4 at the limit. Twilio calls these starting points, not best in class.
  2. Split it: roughly 350 ms to hear, 375 ms to the model's first word, 100 ms to the voice's first sound.
  3. Stream text tokens to the voice as soon as they exist.
  4. Keep answers short. The fastest sentence is the one you didn't say.
  5. Record real calls and time them every week.

WATCH OUTVendor speed claims are marketing until you time your own calls on your own carrier.

Serves: every recipe below

LEVEL 3 / TURN-TAKING / DIFFICULTY ★★★☆☆

Let Them Cut In

INGREDIENTS

  • Turn detection that listens for meaning, not only silence (OpenAI semantic VAD, LiveKit's turn detector, Deepgram Flux)
  • Barge-in: stop talking the moment the caller does
  • A backchannel filter so "uh-huh" isn't an interruption
  • A patient mode for numbers, addresses and emails

STEPS

  1. Turn on interruptions by default.
  2. When the caller cuts in, flush queued audio (Media Streams sends clear).
  3. Trim the agent's memory to what the caller actually heard. ConversationRelay reports it on every interrupt.
  4. Switch to patient turns while collecting a phone number or an order ID.
  5. Make the required disclosures uninterruptible, and nothing else.

WATCH OUTIf the model thinks it finished a sentence the caller never heard, it will refer back to it. Trim the history.

Serves: anyone who talks fast

LEVEL 4 / HUMANS / DIFFICULTY ★★☆☆☆

The Warm Hand-off

INGREDIENTS

  • A short list of triggers: asks for a person, angry, legal or medical question, a refund
  • A transfer tool (SIP refer, LiveKit warm transfer, Vapi or ElevenLabs transfer)
  • A three-line summary for the human before they pick up
  • A fallback for nobody answering

STEPS

  1. Tell the caller you're getting a person, and why.
  2. Brief the human privately first: who, what product, what was already said.
  3. Connect, then leave the call.
  4. If no one picks up, come back, say so and offer a callback.
  5. Count a transfer as done only when a human answered.

WATCH OUTVapi's "forwarded" status means a transfer started, not that anyone answered. Warm modes differ by carrier, and Retell transfers work on phone calls only.

Serves: the hard calls

LEVEL 5 / MULTI-CHANNEL / DIFFICULTY ★★★★☆

Many Doors, One Clerk

INGREDIENTS

  • One agent brain, one catalog tool, one log
  • Doors: phone, web voice (WebRTC), chat, SMS, WhatsApp
  • A QR code on the package that opens the web voice page
  • An MCP server so other people's AI agents can ask too

STEPS

  1. Build the brain once. OpenAI's same model serves browser, server and phone.
  2. Put the QR on the label: it's a phone call with no phone network.
  3. Offer the same catalog lookup as an MCP tool, read-only, for agent visitors.
  4. Keep one conversation ID across doors so a text can continue a call.
  5. Check each door's own rules before you open it.

WATCH OUTWhatsApp business-initiated calls aren't available from US or Canadian business numbers; callers can still ring you. Some carriers and platforms ban whole product categories on SMS.

Serves: every channel, one memory

LEVEL 6 / COMPLIANCE / DIFFICULTY ★★★★☆

The Regulated Kitchen

INGREDIENTS

  • The industry's rulebook, cited section by section
  • An approved answer bank: what the line may say, word for word
  • A refusal list: health claims, dosing advice, anything for minors
  • An age and identity step where the rules ask for one
  • Recording notice and a retention policy

STEPS

  1. Write the rules into tools, not only the prompt: the catalog returns approved text, and the model reads it.
  2. Announce recording at the top of every call. Massachusetts and California require every party's consent.
  3. Route health, legal and adverse-event questions to a trained human, and log them.
  4. Test with hostile callers before launch and review transcripts weekly.
  5. Have counsel read the answer bank once, then again when the rules change.

WATCH OUTExample: Massachusetts cannabis rules (935 CMR 500.105(4)) bar advertising on electronic channels unless 85% of the audience is 21 or older, ban therapeutic claims without evidence, and prohibit marketing gifts, giveaways, discounts, points and loyalty programs. Twilio bans cannabis and CBD messages on US SMS regardless of state law.

Serves: cannabis, health, finance, alcohol

LEVEL 7 / OUTBOUND / DIFFICULTY ★★★★★

Be Called, Properly

INGREDIENTS

  • Prior express written consent, signed, naming the number
  • A Do Not Call scrub: national list and any state list
  • A calling window in the callee's local time
  • An opening line with the business name and a callback number
  • An automated opt-out that works by voice or key press

STEPS

  1. Collect consent first, on its own form, not as a condition of buying anything.
  2. Treat an AI voice as an artificial voice. The FCC ruled in February 2024 that it is one.
  3. Say who is calling at the start. For sales calls, offer the opt-out within two seconds of that.
  4. Call between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. local time federally, and 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. into Massachusetts.
  5. Honor "stop", "cancel", "unsubscribe" and the rest within ten business days, and keep the record.

WATCH OUTMassachusetts also bars unsolicited sales calls made by recorded message device, so an AI sales call into the state without consent is a bad idea. The FCC changed its opt-out ("revoke all") rule in fall 2026; check the current text before launch.

Serves: reminders, follow-ups, members

BONUS STAGE / FORMAT / DIFFICULTY ★★★★★

The Call You Hope to Get

INGREDIENTS

  • A format people already love: Cash Cab's surprise quiz, the radio's "caller number nine"
  • A members list that opted in to be called, in writing
  • One question, under a minute, that a fan can answer
  • Published rules: how winners are picked, the odds, the free way to enter
  • A reward the rules allow in your industry

STEPS

  1. Let members opt in to "the call", and let them leave just as easily.
  2. Call in a window they chose. Open with the brand name, the AI, and "this is the call."
  3. Ask the one question. Right or wrong, make it fun.
  4. Never ask for money, a card number or a fee. Say so out loud.
  5. Reveal the prize in the app or in person, where people can trust it.

WATCH OUTA "you won" phone call is both a prize promotion and an artificial-voice call, and people are trained to distrust it: Publishers Clearing House says it never tells major winners by phone. Real sweepstakes are free. In a regulated industry the prize may be off the table, so the call itself has to be the reward.

Serves: fans

04 / OFFICE POLICY

Six rules taped to the break room fridge.

  • SAY WHO YOU AREBusiness name first, and that it's an AI.
  • SAY IT'S RECORDEDAll-party consent states include Massachusetts and California.
  • ASK BEFORE CALLINGAI voices need consent; sales calls need it in writing.
  • KEEP THE HOURS8 a.m. to 9 p.m. federally. 8 to 8 into Massachusetts.
  • STOP MEANS STOPHonor opt-outs by any reasonable means, fast.
  • NO MONEY ON A PRIZE CALLIf there's a charge, it's a scam. The FTC says so.

The hour, engine by engine

Each bar is one billable hour of model time. Every block is one hard task completed inside it, at the effort level tested. Price the hour, then decide which jobs deserve the expensive tier.

Engine (effort)

Score is the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (higher is better). Meta's Muse Wonder and OpenAI's open gpt-oss models showed no time or cost figures on the board, so they are left out.

The effort dial matters as much as the brand

The same model at a lower effort setting finishes far more tasks per hour for a few points of score. For everyday work, medium or high effort is usually the better buy.

What $20 buys at API prices

Hours of nonstop, flat-out work that twenty dollars pays for at each engine's list price. A $20 monthly plan is not sold this way, but this shows why plans meter you in 5-hour windows and weekly caps.

If you are new to this

Rollout playbook for teams meeting AI in the next 24 months. Treat it like any new labor pool: measure output, cost and error rate, and keep a person accountable for each deliverable.

Try these first

  1. Ask it to explain something from your own job, then check one fact it gives you.
  2. Paste a long email you need to answer and ask for a shorter reply in your voice.
  3. Give it a plan with real limits: a week of dinners under $80, or a Saturday with two kids and a 4 p.m. game.
  4. Upload a lease, a bill or a contract and ask what questions you should ask before signing.
  5. Build one tiny tool: a spreadsheet formula, a checklist, a one-page website.

Good at, today

  • First drafts, summaries and rewrites
  • Explaining a topic at your level
  • Writing and fixing code
  • Brainstorming options and naming things
  • Translating and changing tone

Still weak at

  • Recent facts unless it can search
  • Knowing your situation without being told
  • Admitting it is unsure; it can be wrong in a confident voice
  • Long jobs without checkpoints

Privacy basics

  • Never paste passwords, card numbers or ID numbers.
  • Check the setting that decides whether your chats are used for training.
  • For work data, follow your employer's rule before your own habit.
  • Memory features remember you across chats; you can review and delete what they keep.
  • Assume what you type could be stored.

Five measures worth keeping

  1. Minutes saved per task. Time a job by hand once, then with AI, including the checking.
  2. Cost per hour of model time. The chart above: about $0.70 to $35 depending on engine and effort.
  3. Errors you caught. Keep a rough count for a week. That number tells you how much to trust it.
  4. Your weekly limit. On a plan, the weekly bar is your budget. Spend the top model on the hard jobs.
  5. Who is accountable. A person signs off on anything that goes out. That does not change.

The next two years, and after

Planning outlook. Use these as scenario inputs, not forecasts. Sources are listed below.

The same capability keeps getting cheaper.Epoch AI measured the price of reaching a fixed level of performance falling about 40 times per year, with a range of 9 to 900 times, and noted the fastest drops may not last. Expect today's top-tier hour to cost a fraction as much within two years.
Jobs get longer, not just faster.METR's last published reading had an early Claude Mythos preview finishing, half the time, tasks that take people at least 16 hours, with wide error bars. METR stopped updating that page in September 2026 because its test set ran out of long tasks.
Limits become the unit.Plans already sell 5-hour windows and weekly caps instead of message counts. Expect to budget AI like a phone plan or a utility bill.
Checking becomes the skill.When an hour of model time produces days of draft output, the scarce part is a person who can tell good work from plausible work.
Beyond two years.Compute behaves more like electricity: metered, everywhere, mostly invisible. The durable advantages are the ones a model cannot buy: judgment, taste, trust with people, and knowing your place and your customers well.

How this was measured

  • Tasks per hour is 60 divided by Artificial Analysis's time per task. Cost per hour is its cost per task times tasks per hour. Artificial Analysis derives time per task from the tokens each model used divided by its measured output speed over the past five days, so it reflects API speed, not a stopwatch.
  • A "task" is one weighted Intelligence Index task. These are hard reasoning, coding and agent tasks, so everyday chores finish much faster.
  • Each engine is shown at the effort setting with its highest score, except Fable 5.1, whose xhigh setting outscored max.
  • API prices are the standard-tier list prices on each company's own pricing page, read Nov 8, 2026. Plan wording is quoted as the plan pages put it that day.
  • Numbers on the leaderboard move daily. This sheet holds one reading.

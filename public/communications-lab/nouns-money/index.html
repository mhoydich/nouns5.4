THE BUSINESS OF A SMALL OBJECT

A collection begins
with a clear promise.

One beautiful object. A legible story. A production plan sized to the people who might care.

01

Sell the object, tell the truth.

A collectible note is a piece of art. Its story lives in the image, material and care of making. Edition numbers describe the proposed run; they do not promise scarcity, resale value or returns.

02

A shelf is part of the design.

A studio collection can explain the process. A retail counter needs a clear card and protective packaging. A gallery can add context, with its share visible in the model. Choose the setting before finalising the economics.

03

Recover the run, then learn.

A lower unit cost can hide a larger commitment. Compare total outlay, available copies and the sales needed to recover the full run. Keep the first edition small enough to improve after handling a real proof.

How the model works +

All amounts are illustrative USD assumptions. Fixed work is setup + physical proof + studio labour. Variable cost is printed copies × (paper + printing + finishing + packaging). Contingency applies to their sum. Proofs and archive copies come out of the run and cannot exceed it.

Available copies = printed − retained. Planned sales = floor(available × sell-through). Channel deductions round to the nearest cent for each sold copy. Net receipts = planned sales × (price − deduction per sale). Surplus = net receipts − the entire edition cost. Edition margin = surplus ÷ net receipts; it is undefined when net receipts are zero.

Cash break-even = ceiling(entire edition cost ÷ net receipt per sale). This is recovery of an upfront edition, so unsold and retained copies are already paid for. A zero print run still includes any fixed costs entered. Zero edition cost needs zero sales to recover; positive cost with zero net receipt cannot be recovered. A recovery count above available copies is outside the run.

Material and channel labels describe a concept. They do not automatically replace your rate assumptions. The projection has no volume discounts, actual printer quotes, customer data, orders or payment flow.

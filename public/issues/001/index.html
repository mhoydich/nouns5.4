AN ARGUMENT ABOUT INVITATION

Work needs
better doors.

Most opportunities are signs painted on walls. They announce a mood, request enthusiasm, and leave the actual entry mechanism to whoever is brave enough to ask. A working door is different.

An invitation is an interface.

“Join us.” “Collaborate.” “Get involved.” “We are hiring.” These phrases sound open, but openness without a first move is just atmosphere. The interested person still has to guess whether they belong, what to do, what happens next, who decides, and whether any of it is paid.

That guessing is labor. It favors insiders, professional askers, and people with enough spare time to walk into ambiguity. Everyone else keeps moving—even when they were exactly the person the work needed.

A door is a promise that the wall can change state.

02 / FOUR WORKING PARTS

Put a handle
on the invitation.

  1. 01
    WHO

    Name the person.

    Not a demographic cloud. Describe the judgment, appetite, or lived knowledge the work actually needs.

  2. 02
    FIRST MOVE

    Bound the proof.

    Give someone a small, observable action that creates information without extracting free commercial labor.

  3. 03
    TERMS

    Say what is exchanged.

    Cash, credit, access, ownership, timing, rights, and uncertainty belong on the front of the card.

  4. 04
    YES

    Name the decider.

    A real person accepts the work, replies on a clock, and makes the next state legible—even when the answer is no.

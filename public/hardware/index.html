01 / KEEP THE LAYERS HONEST

Four different
kinds of near.

A mesh is not one magic radio. It is several useful layers that can meet without pretending to be the same thing.

01 / POCKET

Low-rate radio

Short messages, positions, sensor readings, and tiny packets. Long reach; very little bandwidth.

LoRa · Meshtastic · RNode
02 / BETWEEN PLACES

Roof + window links

Wi-Fi-class connections between known points. Line of sight, mounting, and local spectrum rules matter.

OpenWrt · 802.11s · LibreRouter
03 / ON THE BLOCK

Local compute

A small computer serves movies, music, live video, maps, books, games, and bulletin boards nearby.

Kiwix · Owncast · Jellyfin
04 / LOOK UP

Satellite ears

Receive and decode signals from orbit. Start receive-only; transmitting can require licenses and coordination.

TinyGS · SatNOGS
NOT THE SAME THING: A sculptural dish, a LoRa mesh, a Wi-Fi link, and certified satellite internet backhaul are four separate systems.

03 / COMBINE THE LAYERS

A network needs
a reason to exist.

Hardware is the floor. A shared local thing—music, maps, messages, a game, the sky—is what brings people back.

04 / START SMALL ENOUGH TO FINISH

One table.
Two windows.
Three roofs.

  1. 01
    One table

    Host one local page or offline library. Let five people use it. Write down what they came for.

  2. 02
    Two windows

    Measure one link between known places. Log line of sight, signal, power, weather, and permission.

  3. 03
    Three roofs

    Add a redundant path and a second useful service. Name a maintainer before naming a coverage area.

BEFORE YOU MOUNT OR TRANSMIT

Check local radio rules, antenna and roof permissions, safe mounting, grounding, weather exposure, power, privacy, and content rights. Do not describe a hobby network as certified emergency infrastructure.

00 / THE POSITION

The interesting product is not the room. It is what the room knows how to do.

Most people already have the people, the chat, and too much content. What is missing is lightweight social structure: who starts, what happens now, how participation passes, how someone can skip, and what the group gets to keep.

TAG is one wedge—a relay. The larger territory is a layer of small, temporary systems that can live inside a group without turning the group into an audience.

01 / SIGNALS AT THE EDGE

The pieces are arriving.
The protocol is still missing.

These are not competitors to copy. They are evidence that people will enter a tiny social object when it fits the relationship and the moment.

Close-friend photos, reactions without public counts, and recaps make ambient intimacy useful.

02 / TEN WHITE SPACES

Build behavior,
not inventory.

Each card is a credible four-week wedge. The best one is the one a real group asks to restart.

01 / RITUAL LAYER

Protocols, not destinations.

Trigger → act → pass → keep. The product does its work, then disappears back into the group.

Test: can the group explain the rules without you?
02 / GROUP STATE

One live thing everyone can feel.

Who is It? What is open? What expires? A shared state creates tension without a feed.

Test: does unfinished state pull someone back?
03 / ELASTIC PRESENCE

Alive together, tolerant of absence.

Design between synchronous and asynchronous so late arrival changes the play instead of ruining it.

Test: can a round survive a two-hour gap?
04 / THE AFTERLIFE

The recap is the reward.

A round leaves an inside joke, mini-film, playlist, map, or zine—not a leaderboard.

Test: does the group reshare what it made?
05 / ROTATING ROLES

Identity as a temporary job.

Host, challenger, witness, archivist, closer. Give people a reason to act differently for one round.

Test: do quieter people gain a clean entrance?
06 / PORTABLE ROOM

One link across incompatible chats.

Messages, WhatsApp, Discord, Telegram, and SMS can point to the same tiny room without importing contacts.

Test: does it work before account creation?
07 / LOCAL-FIRST SAFETY

Consent is a feature surface.

Skip, pause, soft-exit, delete, export, and group-owned retention should be visible mechanics.

Test: can someone leave without social punishment?
08 / PLACE LAYER

Let geography write the prompt.

Kitchens, blocks, campuses, venues, and festivals create constraints no generic prompt library can.

Test: could this round happen only here?
09 / PLAYABLE PUBLISHING

Release a behavior like an edition.

Artists, DJs, magazines, teachers, and venues author prompt packs, roles, and recaps people perform together.

Test: is the pack recognizable as someone’s voice?
10 / AI BACKSTAGE

Producer, not synthetic friend.

AI can tune pace, vary prompts, translate, and edit recaps. It should not impersonate friends or silently score relationships.

Test: is the group—not the model—the main character?

03 / ECOSYSTEM, NOT APP

A small system has six living layers.

The opportunity grows when creators can author the ritual, groups can own the memory, and many surfaces can carry the same interaction grammar.

  1. 01People

    Friends, families, teams, classmates, neighbors, temporary crews.

  2. 02Roles

    Host, It, witness, DJ, archivist, closer, or a role the group invents.

  3. 03Ritual

    Trigger, turn, time, proof, pass, completion, return.

  4. 04Surface

    Chat extension, room link, widget, lock screen, watch, venue display.

  5. 05Memory

    Recap, playlist, shared album, map, edition, or private archive.

  6. 06Economy

    Creator packs, commissions, licenses, memberships, sponsored rounds—with clear terms.

04 / PRODUCT TANGENTS

The sideways doors are often better.

A

Family + care

A check-in relay, oral-history prompt, medication witness, or “show me your day” recap where warmth matters more than scale.

B

Music

One person passes one song and one instruction. The chain becomes a collectively authored listening room.

C

Creative teams

Critique as a timed pass: notice one thing, make one change, choose the next witness. Less meeting, more visible motion.

D

Events

Pregame, arrival, scavenger layer, photo mission, afterparty recap. The event page becomes playable before and after the date.

E

Campus + neighborhood

Location-bound dares and useful micro-missions that produce a local field guide instead of generic engagement points.

F

Wearables + ambient hardware

A watch tap, widget change, shared light, or venue screen can carry group state without another notification stream.

G

Civic relay

Pass one small act—photograph a broken curb, water a tree, welcome a neighbor—and keep a public proof without surveillance.

H

Playable editions

A magazine issue, album release, exhibition, or brand collaboration becomes a bounded social ritual people can perform and keep.

05 / BUILDER 001

Choose one wedge.
Ship one public proof.

The role is exploratory but the finish is concrete. TAG can be the starting material or you can take an adjacent white space.

  1. WEEK 01One group, one ritual.

    Choose the people first. Run a clickable or facilitated proof in seven days.

  2. WEEK 02Make the handoff work.

    Build the smallest room, state, timer, media, role, or recap the ritual actually needs.

  3. WEEK 03Try three different groups.

    Watch for voluntary restarts, role adoption, soft exits, and what gets reshared.

  4. WEEK 04Publish the system.

    Release the prototype, source, field notes, safety boundaries, and a continue / revise / close call.
